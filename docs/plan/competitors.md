## 1. Utility and telecom bill auditors on contingency

### P3 Cost Analysts

- Serves: organizations "spending more than our category minimums", with examples such as "Large commercial real estate portfolios" and "Local governments & public institutions" ([utility auditing](https://www.costanalysts.com/services/utility-auditing/)). The telecom audit targets organizations that spend "$60K+ annually on IT, software, or telecom" ([telecom auditing](https://costanalysts.com/services/telecom-auditing)).
- Who does the work: P3's own auditors. "We gather invoices, contracts, and a signed LOA" ([utility auditing](https://www.costanalysts.com/services/utility-auditing/)).
- Pricing: "Our service is contingency-based, there's no upfront cost, and fees are tied to verified savings." P3 will "share in the savings each month for a finite period of time." The percentage is not stated publicly ([utility auditing](https://www.costanalysts.com/services/utility-auditing/)).
- Contacts the provider and files claims: yes. "We present findings to utilities, negotiate refunds/credits, and implement the corrections moving forward" ([utility auditing](https://www.costanalysts.com/services/utility-auditing/)). "We work directly with telecom providers to negotiate refunds or credits" ([telecom auditing](https://costanalysts.com/services/telecom-auditing)).
- Time to first refund: "We aim to deliver our initial findings within 30 days and deliver savings within 90 days to your bottom line" ([utility auditing](https://www.costanalysts.com/services/utility-auditing/)).

- Pricing: "Auditel's fee is calculated on a contingency basis, and with no set monthly fee", taking "a portion of the actual telecom invoice savings we deliver monthly". The percentage is not stated publicly ([home page](https://auditelinc.com/)).
- Contacts the provider and files claims: the refund page says a dispute must be filed with the provider and that "Auditel can expedite maximum recovery", but it does not say outright that Auditel files it ([telecom refund](https://auditelinc.com/telecom-audit-services/telecom-recovery-audits/telecom-refund/)).
- Time to first refund: "Although the telecom refund process can take four weeks, sometimes larger refunds take months" ([telecom refund](https://auditelinc.com/telecom-audit-services/telecom-recovery-audits/telecom-refund/)).

### Troy & Banks

## 4. Manual consultants and in-house review

The default for most businesses is that nobody checks the bill, or a staff member or an outside consultant checks it by hand. A guest article in Food Industry Executive says companies "may or may not have the in-house expertise to identify such errors", that catching them needs "knowledgeable and experienced personnel", and that outside auditors are paid in several ways: "Several work on a contingency fee basis with zero front-end costs, some work on an hourly charge basis, and others work on a turnkey project basis" ([Food Industry Executive, June 2023](https://foodindustryexecutive.com/2023/06/are-utility-providers-overbilling-your-company/)). The author is the president of Troy & Banks, an audit firm listed above, so the piece is not neutral.

A UK white paper on telecom audits says "Typically, 85% of a large organisation's telecom invoices are not audited internally, they are simply paid in full" ([The cost of doing nothing, hosted by Crown Commercial Service](https://assets.crowncommercial.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/RM3820-Telecoms-audit-white-paper-v.01.pdf)).

Time to first refund for this category: not stated publicly.

## How Clawback differs

Clawback facts below come from Clawback's own product description and its recorded demo run, not from a third party.

- Customer size. The contingency auditors above describe customers such as local governments, hospitals, real estate portfolios, and firms spending $60K+ a year on telecom ([P3 utility](https://www.costanalysts.com/services/utility-auditing/), [P3 telecom](https://costanalysts.com/services/telecom-auditing), [Auditel](https://auditelinc.com/)). The software tools describe enterprise customers ([Tangoe](https://www.tangoe.com/), [Arcadia](https://www.arcadia.com/), [Cass](https://www.cassinfo.com/utility-bill-management)). Clawback is built for a small business such as a noodle shop.
- Who does the work. The auditors use their own staff, and the software tools use software plus staff. Clawback uses AI agents that read the bill, call the provider, and file the claim, with one SMS approval from the customer.
- Price. Clawback charges 30% of what is actually recovered: the refund once granted, plus first-year savings as they show up on bills. The contingency auditors do not publish their percentage ([P3](https://www.costanalysts.com/services/utility-auditing/), [Auditel](https://auditelinc.com/), [Troy & Banks](https://www.troybanks.com/)). Rocket Money charged 35% to 60% of first-year savings before moving negotiation into Premium+ on November 2, 2026 ([Rocket Money](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/9744474-bill-negotiation-charge)). Billshark charges 40% of savings ([Billshark](https://www.billshark.com/)). The software tools do not publish prices.
- Refunds versus lower rates. The consumer negotiators describe lowering rates ([Rocket Money](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/9744501-how-do-you-lower-my-bill), [Billshark](https://www.billshark.com/)). Clawback looks for overcharges and files refund claims as well as rate savings.
- Speed. P3 aims for findings in 30 days and savings in 90 days ([P3](https://www.costanalysts.com/services/utility-auditing/)). Auditel says refunds can take four weeks to months ([Auditel](https://auditelinc.com/telecom-audit-services/telecom-recovery-audits/telecom-refund/)). Clawback's recorded demo went from start to finished claims in 4 min 28 s. That is time to a filed claim, not time to money received, which still depends on the provider.
